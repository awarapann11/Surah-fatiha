# Surah Al-Fatiha: The Opening of the Quran — Transliteration, Translation, Meaning and Benefits

Surah Al-Fatiha is the first chapter of the Quran, and the most recited passage in a Muslim's life. Seven short verses open the Book, and every prayer begins with them. This guide gives the full Arabic text with transliteration and English translation, explains what each verse means in plain words, and shows how to make the surah a daily habit.

For the complete verse-by-verse page with Urdu translation, see the [Surah Al-Fatiha transliteration](https://currentislam.com/surah-fatiha-transliteration-alhamdulillahi-rabbil-alamin/) guide on Current Islam.

## What Does "Al-Fatiha" Mean?

Al-Fatiha means "The Opening." It opens the Quran, and it opens every prayer. The surah is also called Umm al-Kitab, the Mother of the Book, and Umm al-Quran, the Mother of the Quran, because its seven verses hold the themes of the whole Quran: praise of Allah, worship, and the request for guidance.

It has seven verses. It was revealed in Makkah. A Muslim recites it in every rak'ah of every prayer, which makes it the most repeated chapter of the Quran by far. That is the first reason to learn it properly: the words you say most often deserve the most care.

## What Surah Al-Fatiha Teaches: Three Movements

The seven verses move in three steps, and each step teaches something different.

**Verses 1 to 4: Praise.** The surah opens with Allah's name and His two names of mercy, Ar-Rahman and Ar-Rahim. Then it praises Him as Lord of the worlds and Sovereign of the Day of Recompense. Before asking for anything, the worshipper states who Allah is. Praise comes first, and the request comes later. That order is the whole method of dua in miniature.

**Verse 5: Worship and help.** "It is You we worship and You we ask for help." One verse holds the two halves of a believer's life: worship belongs to Allah alone, and help is asked from Allah alone. The verse uses "You" directly, so the worshipper speaks to Allah, not about Him. This is the centre of the surah, and the sentence that turns praise into a personal conversation.

**Verses 6 to 7: Guidance.** The only request in the surah is for guidance to the straight path, the path of those Allah favoured, not of those who earned anger or went astray. The request is not for wealth, health, or success. It is for direction. Everything else in life depends on walking the right road first.

This three-step shape, praise, then worship, then guidance, is a pattern you can use in any dua. Start with praise, state your worship, then ask. Our guide on [how to make dua](https://currentislam.com/how-to-make-dua/) follows the same order.

## Why Surah Al-Fatiha Is Recited in Every Prayer

No prayer is complete without it. Abu Huraira reported that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "If anyone observes prayer in which he does not recite Umm al-Quran, it is deficient [he said this three times] and not complete." (Sahih Muslim 395a, Book 4, Hadith 41)

The same hadith reports that Allah said: "I have divided the prayer into two halves between Me and My servant, and My servant will receive what he asks." Then it goes through the surah verse by verse: when the servant says "Praise be to Allah, the Lord of the universe," Allah says, "My servant has praised Me." When the servant asks for the straight path, Allah says, "This is for My servant, and My servant will receive what he asks for."

That is why the surah is not a formality before prayer. It is the prayer itself, in its shortest form. A person who recites it slowly, knowing what each verse means, is already doing what the prayer is for: praising Allah, declaring worship, and asking for guidance.

## The Greatest Surah in the Quran

Abu Sa'id Al-Mu'alla narrated that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said to him: "Shall I not teach you the most superior Surah in the Quran?" He said: "(It is), 'Praise be to Allah, the Lord of the worlds' (i.e., Surat Al-Fatiha) which consists of seven repeatedly recited Verses and the Magnificent Quran which was given to me." (Sahih al-Bukhari 5006, Book 66, Hadith 28)

"Seven repeatedly recited verses" is the description the hadith itself gives. The surah is short enough to memorise in one sitting, and it is repeated so often that it stays with a person for life. That combination, short and constantly repeated, is what makes it the foundation of the Quran for ordinary Muslims, not scholars alone.

## The Opening Loop: A Daily Habit

A habit needs a trigger, a short action, and a moment to notice what changed. Here is a three-step loop built from the surah itself.

**Pause.** Before Fajr, or before you pick up your phone in the morning, stop for ten seconds. No screen, no talking. The pause is the trigger.

**Praise.** Recite Surah Al-Fatiha once, slowly, in Arabic if you can, or read the transliteration. Mean verse 2 as you say it: all praise belongs to Allah, Lord of the worlds. One slow recitation takes about thirty seconds.

**Ask.** After the recitation, ask for one thing for the day ahead in your own words. Guidance for a decision, patience with a difficult person, steadiness in work. One request, not a list. Then start the day.

Repeat the loop after Fajr or before sleep, and it becomes a habit in days, not months. Pair it with other daily recitations if you already have them, such as [Manzil Dua](https://currentislam.com/manzil-dua/), and keep the Fatiha loop itself short so it survives busy days.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Surah Al-Fatiha obligatory in prayer?**

Yes. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said that a prayer without Umm al-Quran is deficient and not complete (Sahih Muslim 395a). It is recited in every rak'ah. A person praying behind an imam recites it inwardly.

**What does "Alhamdulillahi Rabbil Alamin" mean?**

It means "[All] praise is [due] to Allah, Lord of the worlds." It is verse 2 of the surah. "Alhamdu" is praise, "Rabb" is Lord, the One who creates, owns, and cares for everything, and "alamin" is the worlds, everything besides Allah.

**How many verses are in Surah Al-Fatiha?**

Seven verses. They are the seven repeatedly recited verses named in Sahih al-Bukhari 5006.

**Can I recite Surah Al-Fatiha in English?**

You can read the translation to understand it, but the prayer itself is recited in Arabic. If you cannot read Arabic yet, learn the transliteration first, verse by verse, and check your pronunciation with a teacher or a reliable recitation.
